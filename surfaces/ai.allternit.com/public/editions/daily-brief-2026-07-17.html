Security agent evaluation is finally shifting from CTF success-rate leaderboards to cost-aware tradeoffs. Kassianik et al. make the case that measuring peak offensive capability under generous inference budgets ignores the economics of real attacks and defenses, so production teams should evaluate agents by cost per compromise and defender mitigation cost, not just exploit completion.

Research

  • Partition, Prompt, Aggregate: Statistical Self-Consistency in Language Models

Wolf et al. treat in-context learning as conditional expectation estimation and propose splitting the prompt into subsets, eliciting model outputs from each, and aggregating the results. This produces a more consistent estimator of the target conditional distribution and reduces dependence on any single prompt ordering or phrasing. For production teams, it offers a way to improve few-shot reliability and uncertainty calibration without fine-tuning or larger models — though it trades extra inference calls for stability.

  • Beyond Success Rate: Cost-Aware Evaluation of Offensive and Defensive Security Agents

Kassianik, Nelson, and Singer argue that security-agent benchmarks overstate capability by granting unlimited inference budget for vulnerability discovery, exploit development, and CTF completion. They propose evaluating agents under realistic cost constraints, accounting for both attacker spend and defender mitigation cost. If you are building, buying, or hardening autonomous red-team tools, this reframes the buying decision: a lower success rate at a dramatically lower cost can still be a viable attack vector, and your defensive ROI should be measured accordingly.
